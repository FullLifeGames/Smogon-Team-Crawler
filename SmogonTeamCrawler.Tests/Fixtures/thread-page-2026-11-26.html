ArceusGroudon-PrimalHo-OhZygardeFezandipitiDeoxys-Attack (click for team)
In a way, Ebony God gets to show off two of his Arceus sets he's been cooking up this cycle because Choice Specs Arceus was also an idea that I'm pretty sure he first came up with. Specs Arceus is actually very scary in a manner similar to Lunala where there's very little that's comfortable taking a hit from it. For example, after Terastalizing, Timid has a 98% chance to 2HKO your standard Offensive Ho-Oh. Thanks to Judgment being 16 PP it's also not terrified to actually click the button that does damage, and like its contemporaries in Eternatus and Lunala it has amazing coverage and pretty good bulk. For its other moves, Shadow Ball was a no-brainer, as was something to handle the Steels. Earth Power was tempting to do more to Primal Groudon and Eternatus before Terastalizing, but I ultimately chose Overheat instead for its ability to OHKO Zacian. There were other benefits to it too, such as threatening Ferrothorn and hitting Marshadow for solid damage in case you aren't sure if they're switch in it or Zacian, and ideally I'd Terastalize this most of the time anyways. There were plenty of choices for the last slot, but Extreme Speed stood out for the extra insurance against fast sweepers which I feel that this team appreciates very much. It also does more damage to Chansey. For the EV spread, I was tempted by Modest but ultimately decided getting the jump on other Arceus was of greater importance. After a bit of tinkering, I found that 104 HP and 4 SpD allowed me to survive a +1 Tera Dragon Dynamax Cannon after rocks, and the leftover EV's give me just enough points to put into speed to where I can reasonably expect that no Arceus is going to out-speed creep me unless they're fully investing into speed.

I immediately sought a strong defensive core to support Arceus, and ended up gravitating towards the classic trio of Groudon, Ho-Oh, and Zygarde for their generally comprehensive cohesion. After this, I decided I wanted a pivot. I was heavily considering just running Alomomola alongside these Pokemon because without it Arceus sounded like it might struggle heavily with longevity, but that was ultimately a dead end. Since my Arceus was both quite frail for an Arceus and didn't check Ultra Necrozma, any variation of the team either wasn't defensively sound enough or was just too passive. As such, Fezandipiti was the obvious choice for its defensive capabilities and ability to do many of the things Arceus would normally do had it not decided to put on the funny glasses. The last slot was difficult to decide. I wanted another Pokemon with defensive utility to deal with the many defensive traits my team still lacked, but after thinking for a while I ultimately found that the way to go was to add Deoxys Attack and that I had other ways to deal with the perceived vulnerability of this team.

I needed an Ice resist so Chien-Pao wouldn't make mincemeat of this team, so I put Tera Water on Zygarde. Since this removed its ability to check Dragon Dance Zygarde, I gave Fezandipiti Alluring Voice. For a bit I was worried that I'd have to drop Extreme Speed on Arceus to have something to take care of Garganacl, but then I realized I can just win the PP war with Zygarde. Of course, there was one Pokemon looming over all of this like an anvil. Without a Dark type, Ultra Necrozma posed a huge threat to my team. Fortunately, I believe I have developed a reliable strategy against it. In most cases, the Pokemon it will set up on are either Fezandipiti or an Arceus locked into a normal move. For both of these, the opponent will usually keep their Ultra Necrozma as NDM as they set up. Because they can't Ultra Burst and use Light That Burns the Sky on the same turn, this allows me to switch in Zygarde and, provided it is healthy, paralyze the threat before it can get out of hand. In the event that they do Ultra Burst on the first turn however, things get more dicey. I do have Alluring Voice on Fezandipiti, but realistically I'm never going to actually call out the Ultra Burst and click it and even if I did that wouldn't do that much anyways. For this reason, I decided to change Zygarde's Tera type again, this time to Steel. While long term it leaves Zygarde vulnerable to many of the things it usually walls, for the short term it can be used to parry attacks such as Light That Burns the Sky and Dynamax Cannon while also possessing the same resistances to Steel and Ice as Water. Yes, this does now leave Zygarde vulnerable to Sacred Sword from Chien-Pao, but I think most people don't actually use that anyways and even if they do even a super effective Sacred Sword only does 55-65% to base Zygarde, and clicking it is risky and something I can take advantage of. I need to stress that Sacred Sword is actually way, way less scary than it looks like at first. I also went back and forth on Tera Dark for Ho-Oh as a last resort against Ultra Necrozma but ultimately decided that Tera Grass would generally be better to be able to handle Primal Kyogre in more positions.

All in all, I think this team is quite swell. You've heard this story before. I don't know how to say "this team is good defensively and offensively" in new ways every single time, but I do think this team can consistently handle whatever comes its way and retaliate with devastating attacks from Arceus and Deoxys Attack with pivot support.

DD Rayquaza HO Arceus Rayquaza Deoxys-Speed Eternatus Zacian-Crowned Groudon-Primal

Tera Ghost Arceus is always a solid option on any hyper offense team, but that's not really the main selling point of this team. Arceus is here because it has really good offensive overlap with Rayquaza. While Mega Salamence is the more consistent Flying-type for hazard stack Hyper Offense teams, and Yveltal offers Sucker Punch priority, Rayquaza's sheer power with Life Orb and especiallly Tera Flying makes it deadly for most fat balances and stall teams to handle reliably, especially since it has a free moveslot to use Substitute with against Rocky Helmet Alomomola and against Tera Steel Arceus-Dark. Earthquake is preferred over V-Create to bully Garganacl and to avoid losing Speed. Initially when making this I thought Rayquaza's niche over Mega Salamence would be Extreme Speed, and while it is nice, if you're not Swords Dance + Tera Normal, then this is a pretty weak move against most offensive threats, especially since you can't afford Adamant due to underspeeding Zacian-C (unless you use Dragon Dance on Sticky Web).

Anyways, rest of the team is pretty standard. Eternatus absorbs Toxic Spikes, with Life Orb + Recover is preferred to make Marshadow and Choice Scarf Yveltal MU easier. Zacian-C is a staple on HO against opposing Eternatus, Arceus, and Arceus-Fairy for Rayquaza. Primal Groudon overwhelms Zygarde for Rayquaza and Arceus while helping against opposing Zacian-C.

Mewtwo-Mega-YFezandipitiGroudon-PrimalZygardeHo-OhMarshadow (click for team)
I haven't built with this Pokemon in a while so I wanted to throw my hat in the ring. Of course I started with Mewtwo. Running Ice Beam because you'd have to hold a gun to my head to get me to use Focus Blast when I don't need to. The analysis has Unnerve but that's bogus because I'm genuinely unsure if there's a single Pokemon that actually runs berries at this point in the meta and even for the ones that might the only possible scenario where it could matter is Lum Berry triggering against the status proc of Fire Blast or Ice Beam which are not moves I'll be clicking against Arceus very often, let alone in base, whereas draining Sucker Punch PP with Pressure is actually something that matters.

The teammates I immediately sought out were Fezandipti and the standard balance staples. A core idea of this team was the gameplan of pivoting from Fezandipiti into Mewtwo, particularly powerful against the terrifying Eternatus. This is where the troubles with building this team first started to pop up. Dragon Dance Arceus Ground seemed like it would be a good choice here but in many ways it kinda also defeated much of the point of using Mewtwo so I didn't want to. I wanted the team to be more offensive so I made Primal Groudon Utility Swords Dance. I also began to heavily consider Spikes over Stealth Rock to provide more pressure to much of the scariest Pokemon to this team, an idea I ultimately ran with.

Then, I made the single most pivotal decision for this team. I made Ho-Oh Choice Band. There were many upsides to this. In general it synergized well with Fezandipiti and Mewtwo, and much of what I was worried about checking this Pokemon would be great at checking offensively. In general it made this team quite scary. However, it also introduced a plethora of weaknesses to the team that I now had to handle with a single slot. I needed to maintain and improve a gameplan for Zacian, have a switch in for Primal Kyogre, ideally provide more offensive pressure, and also provide hazard support. This was a tall task and stumped me for a while. The overlap between defogger and Primal Kyogre switch in is basically just Arceus formes, and needing to spend a moveslot on Defog made them all too passive and let something, usually Zacian, take advantage of them. I was really attached to Choice Band Ho-Oh and wasn't willing to part with it, but it was clear something had to change, and no combination of sixth Pokemon and tweaks to its teammates would pick up the slack for Ho-Oh.

...But what if Ho-Oh was the one to change? An idea dawned on me. An idea so stupid it just might work. The problem arised from Ho-Oh's Choice Band meaning I now had to look for both a Defogger and a switch-in to Primal Kyogre. But what if Ho-Oh was my switch in to Primal Kyogre? This idea was insanely risky. Needing to burn Tera on Ho-Oh essentially whenever the opponent has a Primal Kyogre was something that would leave me very vulnerable and leave me with few answers if I were to be put in a bad position. But it could work. In fact, it could work quite well considering Ho-Oh threatens an OHKO on Wallbreaker. Consistently Terastalizing to Grass also gets rid of Ho-Oh's crippling weakness to Stealth Rock. Ho-Oh could at minimum force Primal Kyogre out multiple times, and that's about the most you can hope for against this Pokemon. It didn't even need to switch into Primal Groudon for this, allowing it to stay healthy to deal with Zacian and/or be more comfortable staying in, allowing it to be used more and set more hazards. And even in scenarios where I can't Tera Ho-Oh, I still have Fezandipiti as an emergency switch-in, now with Tera Water to both resist Water attacks from Primal Kyogre and provide an Ice resist for Chien-Pao if needed. This was it. I did it. I shot down the thing keeping me from completing this team and making it defensively sound. I no longer needed a Primal Kyogre switch in, and didn't even need to worry about Zacian that much anymore now that the burden on Primal Groudon was lightened. I could have slot in something like Defensive Arceus Ground for hazard control and have a complete team. That would be the safe move. That would be the smart move. That would be what should be done in that scenario. However, at this point, I had decided something.

Fuck it. We ball.

The last Pokemon on this team is Marshadow. Another powerful wallbreaker that would make this team insanely threatening in addition to the priority support it provides the team. Of course there's a very obvious problem this has. This team has no hazard control. This is a crippling weakness for a balance team. So then, this will not be a balance team, at least not a typical one. I've been experimenting with more volatile teams recently, but perhaps more than any other team I've made, this team requires excellent positioning to not fall apart. The difference is that this applies just as much against other balance teams as it does Hyper Offense. Constant pressure needs to be applied to make sure the opponent can't last long enough to wear this team down with hazards. While this is definitely something this team can do, and Toxic Spikes can be absorbed by Fezandipiti, this team cannot allow the opponent to gain momentum and need to be constantly making progress, pivoting into the big threats at regular intervals and key moments while also preserving the Pokemon needed for the future. But despite the precipice this team teeters on, I think it definitely has what it takes. This team still has defensive answers to allow it to reposition, but it also is offensively threatening enough to enable it to speed up the pace of the game and force trades and ensure that nothing the opponent accomplishes comes for free.

A prime example of this is this team's game plan against Zacian. A Primal Groudon that is usually healthy allows for a fairly consistent answer to Zacian, but if enough pressure is applied to where this can't be a reliable answer there exists a second strategy: stand your ground. Zacian is afforded very few opportunities to properly sweep this team. Often, it relies on forcing things out to get momentum going, but this team is designed with the ability to call its bluff and consistently punish it. The best case scenario for Zacian here is that Ho-Oh is locked into Brave Bird which, mind you, still does at minimum 40% and may put it into range of being killed by Shadow Sneak after one round of Spikes chip. Fezandipiti might not be able to threaten it itself, but even Ho-Oh is fast enough to revenge kill it after a speed drop. As such, this team can force Zacian to either be punished for its insolence or take out whatever is in front of it and let in Primal Groudon or Ho-Oh. And rest assured that without its Attack boost, Zacian will not be able to be as threatening the next time it comes in and will likely not have enough power to protect it. This is a sufficient backup plan and is in addition to Zygarde who will often be able to take a hit from even +3 Zacian. This team is more than prepared for this Pokemon, and yet this is one of its scariest matchups actually.

I understand if the, uh, quirks of this team puts you off, but I'm proud of what I have created and think it's quite capable. It's ability to adapt and switch between bashing through the foe with its powerful wallbreakers and falling back on its defensive core to react and reposition gives it insane flexibility, and if you're good at positioning you can take control of the game and play at the pace the opponent is least suited to deal with. And even in its worst matchups, the amount of options and ways to advance the game state this team has makes it hard to lock down and plan for in its entirety. I'm quite happy with my teambuilding, and am proud to show it off here.
